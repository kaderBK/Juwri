# juwri Acceptable Use Policy

Version 1.0 · Effective 4 October 2026

This Acceptable Use Policy ("**AUP**") applies to every Customer and User of the juwri Cloud
Services. It forms part of the juwri Subscription Terms ("**Terms**") between **JUWRI LTD** (company
number 14363043, England and Wales) ("**juwri**", "**we**", "**us**") and you ("**Customer**",
"**you**"). Capitalised words not defined here have the meaning in the Terms. If this AUP and the
Terms conflict, the Terms prevail.

The Services are used by restaurants, cafés, shops, hotels, pharmacies, laundries and other
businesses to take orders, run point-of-sale, message customers and publish content. Most of what
you do is fine. This AUP says what is not, and what is allowed only on conditions. It is not a list
of everything the law requires of you: you remain responsible for your own compliance (Terms 3.2).

## 1. Who and what this covers

1.1 **What is covered.** This AUP covers all use of the Cloud Services, including storefronts, QR
menus, point-of-sale, kitchen screens, delivery, reservations, loyalty, coupons, messaging and
marketing campaigns, link-in-bio pages, the blog and page builder, AI features, the marketplace and
the Partner Relay. It covers everything you or your Users upload, publish, send, sell or generate
("**Content**"), and the products and services you offer through the Services.

1.2 **Your Users and customers.** You are responsible for your Users. You must make sure they follow
this AUP, and you must act when someone using your store, page or messaging breaks it.

1.3 **Resellers and white-label partners.** A reseller or white-label partner must follow this AUP
and must pass equivalent rules to the customers it hosts. The Reseller Agreement may add stricter
rules.

1.4 **Self-hosted software.** This AUP does not apply to your use of the AGPL Software or the
Enterprise Software on your own infrastructure. It does apply to any Cloud Services you use with
them, such as AI credits, messaging bundles, the Partner Relay and hosted pages.

## 2. Prohibited content and activities

You must not use the Services to create, publish, sell, send, store or promote any of the following.

### 2.1 Illegal goods and services

- Anything that is illegal to sell or offer where you operate or where your customer receives it,
  including illegal drugs and the equipment to make or use them, stolen goods, and goods or services
  that need a licence you do not hold.
- Counterfeit or "replica" goods, grey-market goods imported or exported against the law, and goods
  made with forced or child labour.
- Human trafficking, exploitation, or services that facilitate them.
- Sexual services, escort services and pornography (see also 3.9).
- Cryptocurrency mining, token sales, and unlicensed money transmission, exchange or investment
  schemes (see also 3.8).

### 2.2 Intellectual property

- Content that infringes anyone's copyright, trade mark, design, patent or other rights, including
  using another business's name, logo, photographs, menus or reviews without permission.
- Pretending to be an official partner of, or affiliated with, a delivery platform, payment
  provider, brand or authority when you are not.

We act on valid notices of infringement under section 10.

### 2.3 Harmful and abusive content

- Child sexual abuse material or any sexualised content involving children. We report it to the
  authorities and remove it at once, without notice.
- Content that promotes, praises or helps violent extremism or terrorism, or the groups behind them.
- Hate speech: content that attacks or demeans people because of race, ethnicity, nationality,
  religion, sex, gender, sexual orientation, disability or similar characteristics.
- Threats, incitement to violence, harassment, doxxing, or content that encourages self-harm.
- Content that is defamatory, or that shares private information about a person without a lawful
  reason.

### 2.4 Fraud, phishing and deception

- Fraud, scams, pyramid or multi-level-marketing schemes, advance-fee schemes and get-rich-quick
  offers.
- Phishing, impersonating a person, business or authority, or collecting passwords, card details or
  one-time codes under false pretences.
- Fake stores, bait-and-switch offers, or taking payment for goods or services you do not intend to
  supply.
- Fake or misleading reviews, ratings and testimonials (see section 7).
- Money laundering, or using a store to move funds unconnected with real sales.

### 2.5 Malware and technical abuse

- Viruses, malware, ransomware, cryptominers, spyware, or code designed to damage, hijack or gain
  unauthorised access to any system or device.
- Hosting, redirecting to or linking to malicious sites.
- Using the Services to attack others, for example as a relay, a botnet component or a stolen-card
  testing tool ("carding").

### 2.6 Abuse of the platform

- Scraping, crawling or harvesting data from the Services, other customers' storefronts or the
  marketplace, except through our published APIs and within their rate limits.
- Overloading or degrading the Services, including load testing without our written permission.
- Creating accounts to avoid a suspension, a limit or a fee, or to get free-plan or promotional
  benefits repeatedly.
- Interfering with or bypassing authentication, tenant separation, usage limits, rate limits, AI
  credit metering, plan limits or billing.
- Using the Services to build a competing product, or to copy or reverse-engineer closed parts of
  the Cloud Services, except as the AGPL Software licence or the law allows.
- Sending traffic through the Partner Relay that is not genuine orders, menus or status updates for
  your own stores.

### 2.7 Licence Keys and resale

- Sharing, selling, altering, extending or bypassing a Licence Key or the licence check, or running
  Enterprise Software for more Accounts or features than your Order allows (Terms 2.3 and 2.4).
- Reselling, sub-licensing or giving access to the Cloud Services, or offering them under your own
  brand, unless you have signed a Reseller Agreement with us. Using the Services to run stores for
  other businesses without a Reseller Agreement is resale.

### 2.8 Security testing

- Testing, scanning or probing the Services or other customers' stores for weaknesses, other than
  under the rules and safe harbour at https://juwri.com/security. In short: test only your own
  installation or your own free test account, keep automated tools under the stated request rate, do
  not access other people's data, and report what you find through {{SECURITY_REPORT}}.
- Social-engineering our staff or customers, or physical attacks.

## 3. Restricted businesses and products

The goods and services below are allowed only if you meet every condition stated. If a condition is
not met, the product must not be listed. We may ask for evidence at any time, including before a
store is published, and we may switch off, hide or unpublish a category, a product or a store that
does not meet them.

For all restricted goods: the law of the place where you sell and where the customer receives the
goods applies, and the stricter rule governs. Where the law of either place forbids the sale, it is
forbidden on juwri. We may refuse or limit a category in a country even if it is lawful there, for
example because a payment provider, messaging provider or delivery partner does not allow it.

### 3.1 Pharmacies and medicines

Pharmacy selling is available only in the countries where juwri has enabled it for pharmacy stores.
The enabled countries are shown in your dashboard. A pharmacy store cannot be published in a country
that is not enabled.

(a) **Only licensed pharmacies.** You may sell medicines only if you are a pharmacy or other
business licensed to do so where you operate, and you hold, and keep valid, every licence and
approval required, including any approval to sell online or to deliver.

(b) **Evidence before publishing.** Before a pharmacy store is published, you must upload your
licence documents and the details the store setup asks for (licence number, issuing authority,
expiry date, responsible pharmacist where required). The details shown on your store must be
accurate. You must upload renewed documents before they expire. We may keep a pharmacy store
unpublished until we have reviewed them.

(c) **Over-the-counter only.** You may sell only non-prescription ("over-the-counter" or "general
sale") medicines and health products that are lawful to sell online in the country of sale. You must
never offer, take orders for, advertise or arrange the supply of:
- prescription-only medicines (including by uploading a prescription through your store);
- controlled substances, narcotics, psychotropic medicines or anything classed as such;
- unregistered, unlicensed, counterfeit, expired or unapproved medicines;
- medicines that must be dispensed only face to face or only by a pharmacist.

(d) **No promotions where forbidden.** You must not run discounts, coupons, bundles, free gifts,
loyalty rewards or marketing campaigns for medicines where the law or regulator of that market
forbids or restricts them. Where a market forbids it, we may turn off promotion tools for pharmacy
stores in that market.

(e) **Advertising.** Medicine advertising must follow the local approval and content rules, must not
make claims you cannot support, and must not use fear or promise cures.

(f) **Country rules.** The table below is a summary of rules we understand to apply today. It is not
a complete statement, and it does not replace advice for your own business. You must follow the
current law of each country where you sell.

| Country | Summary |
|---|---|
| United Kingdom | The pharmacy must be registered with the General Pharmaceutical Council and follow its rules for services provided at a distance. Prescription-only medicines are not permitted on juwri in any event. |
| Saudi Arabia | Prescription-only medicines cannot be offered through distance selling; controlled medicines are prohibited; medicine advertising needs prior SFDA approval. |
| Egypt | Medicines that need a prescription need a valid prescription from a licensed physician, dispensed by a licensed pharmacy, and medicine advertising needs Ministry of Health approval. |

For any other country, the pharmacy settings shown in your dashboard apply, and you must follow the
law there.

### 3.2 Alcohol

(a) You may sell or promote alcohol only where it is lawful and only if you hold every licence the
law requires for the sale and, where relevant, the delivery.

(b) **Saudi Arabia and Kuwait.** The sale of alcohol is prohibited in Kuwait and, apart from a
narrow diplomatic-only arrangement, in Saudi Arabia. Stores that sell, list or promote alcohol to
customers in either country are not allowed on juwri.

(c) **United Kingdom.** You need the premises licence and personal licence the Licensing Act
requires, and an age-verification policy. You must carry out age checks at sale and on delivery, and
must not sell to anyone under 18.

(d) Alcohol content must not target minors, must not encourage excess, and must not be shown in
messaging to anyone who has not confirmed they are of legal age. Alcohol is not allowed in WhatsApp
messaging in countries where the WhatsApp policy does not allow it (see section 6).

### 3.3 Tobacco, vaping and nicotine

(a) You may sell tobacco, e-cigarettes, e-liquids, nicotine pouches and related products only where
lawful and with every licence and registration required.

(b) You must check the buyer's age, and must not sell to anyone under the legal age.

(c) You must follow local packaging, health-warning, display, flavour and advertising rules. In the
United Kingdom, the Tobacco and Vapes Act 2026 introduces a generational sales ban (nobody born on
or after 1 January 2009 may be sold tobacco), a retail licensing scheme and limits on vape
advertising, with different provisions starting on different dates.

(d) Promotions, free samples and discounts on tobacco and nicotine are not allowed where the law
forbids them.

### 3.4 Other age-restricted goods

Where the law sets a minimum age for a product (for example knives, solvents, fireworks, energy
drinks sold to children, or lottery products), you must check the buyer's age at sale or delivery
and must not sell to under-age buyers. If your store cannot check age properly, you must not list
the product. You must not use Services features such as quick re-order or scheduled orders to get
around an age check.

### 3.5 CBD and cannabis products

Cannabis, THC products and any product that is illegal where the customer is must not be sold. CBD
products are allowed only if they comply with local law, including any limit on THC content and any
novel-food or licensing rule, and only in countries where they are lawful. Because CBD is illegal or
tightly controlled in most of the Gulf and Egypt, juwri may restrict CBD to the United Kingdom only.

### 3.6 Weapons

You must not sell firearms, ammunition, explosives, or their parts, replicas that can be converted,
or any weapon whose sale is illegal. Lawful knives, tools or sporting goods may be sold only with
age checks and any licence or delivery restriction the law imposes.

### 3.7 Gambling, lotteries and raffles

Online gambling, betting, casino games, sweepstakes and paid prize draws are not allowed. You may
run a free promotional prize draw or competition only if it is lawful where you run it, its rules
are published, entry does not depend on a purchase or payment where the law forbids that, and any
licence the law requires is held.

### 3.8 Financial services

You must not use the Services to offer loans, credit, investments, insurance, money transfer,
foreign exchange, crypto-assets, debt collection or debt relief, unless you hold the licence the law
requires and we have approved it in writing. Accepting payment for your own goods through your own
payment gateway is not a financial service.

### 3.9 Adult content

Pornography, sexually explicit content, adult entertainment and sex-related products are not
allowed. Nudity or sexualised imagery must not appear in storefronts, pages, messaging or
AI-generated Content.

### 3.10 Health claims and supplements

(a) You must not say or imply that a product treats, cures or prevents a disease unless the law
allows that claim and you can prove it.

(b) Supplements, vitamins, weight-loss and sports-nutrition products must follow the labelling,
registration and advertising rules of the country of sale, and must not be advertised to children.

(c) You must not offer medical services, diagnosis or telemedicine through the Services unless you
are licensed to do so and we have approved it in writing.

### 3.11 Live animals

You must not sell live animals or animal parts of protected species. You may sell pet food and
supplies.

### 3.12 Other regulated categories

Other goods and services regulated in your market (for example fireworks, hazardous chemicals, car
parts, second-hand electronics) need the licences and checks the law requires. If we tell you a
category needs extra evidence, you must provide it.

## 4. Food, allergens and halal

4.1 **Allergen information.** You must give accurate allergen information for the food you sell, and
keep it up to date when recipes or suppliers change. In the United Kingdom, rules require allergen
information for all food, and full ingredient and allergen labelling on food that is prepacked for
direct sale (the rule often called Natasha's Law). Other countries have their own rules. Do not rely
on AI-generated or imported descriptions for allergens without checking them.

4.2 **Content you control.** Allergen fields, "may contain" notices and dietary labels (vegan,
vegetarian, gluten-free) in your store are your statements. We provide the fields; we do not check
the facts.

4.3 **Halal and other certified claims.** You may describe food as halal, kosher, organic or similar
only if you hold the certification or can prove the claim to the standard required where you sell.
You must not use a certification mark you do not hold.

4.4 **Food safety.** You must hold the food-hygiene registration or permit the law requires, and
must not use the Services to sell food you know to be unsafe.

## 5. Pricing and honest selling

5.1 **Prices.** Prices you show must be accurate and must match what the customer pays. Where the
law requires prices to be shown including VAT or similar tax (for example for consumer sales in
Saudi Arabia), you must set your store to show tax-inclusive prices. You are responsible for
choosing the correct setting.

5.2 **No hidden charges.** You must show delivery fees, service charges, tips and other mandatory
charges before the customer pays.

5.3 **Offers.** "Was/now" prices, countdowns, "limited stock" claims and discounts must be true. You
must not use fake scarcity, fake urgency or fake price cuts.

5.4 **Terms of sale.** You must show your refund, cancellation, delivery and complaint terms where
the law requires, and must honour them.

5.5 **Fiscal documents.** You must not use the Services to avoid tax, falsify invoices or receipts,
or issue documents that do not match the sale. Where e-invoicing is mandatory (for example ZATCA in
Saudi Arabia), you must use it properly.

## 6. Messaging rules

These rules apply to every SMS, WhatsApp, email, push, in-app and other message you send through the
Services, including marketing campaigns, order updates, loyalty and coupon messages.

6.1 **Consent for marketing.** You must have valid consent for each recipient before sending
marketing, promotional or loyalty messages, in the form the law of the recipient's country requires.
You must keep a record of when, how and for what each consent was given, and give it to us on
request.

6.2 **Order and service messages.** Messages that are necessary to deliver an order the customer has
placed (confirmation, status, delivery, receipt) are not marketing, but must not contain promotions.
Where the same message combines the two, it is treated as marketing.

6.3 **Easy opt-out.** Every marketing message must tell the recipient how to stop receiving
messages, in the same channel and in the recipient's language where possible, free of charge. You
must stop within the time the law sets, and in any case promptly, and you must not message anyone
who has opted out unless they opt in again. The Services record opt-outs; you must not override
them.

6.4 **No purchased or scraped lists.** You must not message people whose details you bought, rented,
scraped, harvested, or got from another business without their specific consent to hear from you.

6.5 **Honest sender.** You must identify your business as the sender, must not disguise your
identity, must not use a misleading sender name or number, and must use only sender IDs you are
entitled to use.

6.6 **Quiet hours.** You must not send marketing messages at times when the law, a regulator or the
carrier forbids or discourages it. Unless a local rule is stricter, you should avoid sending
marketing messages late at night or early in the morning in the recipient's local time.

6.7 **WhatsApp.** You must follow the WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy and the Meta rules that
apply to you, including: getting opt-in before contacting someone; using approved message templates
to start conversations outside the customer-service window; offering opt-out; not sending prohibited
content; and not selling goods or services Meta does not allow. Meta can limit or disable your
number, and that is outside our control. We may block templates or campaigns that we think will
breach the policy.

6.8 **SMS sender IDs.** Where a country requires sender IDs to be registered or marked (for example
in the United Arab Emirates), you must use the correct sender ID, and you must give us the
registration documents we need.

6.9 **Country summaries.** The table below is a summary of the rules as we understand them, not
legal advice.

| Country | Summary |
|---|---|
| United Kingdom | PECR: you need specific consent to market by email or text, except to existing customers for similar products where you offered an opt-out when you collected their details and in every message. Every message needs a simple way to unsubscribe. Calls and texts to numbers on the TPS may also be restricted. |
| Saudi Arabia | CST (formerly CITC) anti-spam rules: promotional messages need prior consent, a free and easy opt-out, and stop after an opt-out within the time the regulator sets. |
| United Arab Emirates | TDRA marketing SMS policy: consent first, and a clear way to opt in and out. |

Other countries have their own rules on marketing messages, and you must follow them.

## 7. Reviews and marketing honesty

7.1 You must not write, buy, trade for or ask others to write fake reviews, ratings, stars or
testimonials, and must not post or use reviews that you know, or should know, are false or
misleading.

7.2 You must not offer money, discounts, points or other rewards for reviews unless you clearly
disclose it, and you must not reward only positive reviews. You must not hide or delete genuine
negative reviews while keeping positive ones, in a way that misleads customers.

7.3 If the Services let you collect or display reviews, you must take reasonable steps to prevent
and remove reviews that are fake.

7.4 You must not make claims you cannot support, use another business's reviews as your own, or use
endorsements without permission.

## 8. AI features

8.1 **Review before publishing.** AI output can be wrong (Terms 5.4). You must review all
AI-generated Content before you publish or send it, especially prices, allergens, ingredients,
health claims, legal statements and anything about a person.

8.2 **No deception.** You must not use AI features to create fake reviews, fake testimonials, fake
news, deepfakes, or content designed to mislead people about who made it or what it is. You must not
use AI features to impersonate a real person or business, or to pretend a person wrote or said
something they did not.

8.3 **Tell people when needed.** Where the law, a platform policy or a reasonable customer would
expect to know that they are speaking to an automated agent or reading AI-generated content, you
must tell them. You must not claim that an AI agent is a human.

8.4 **Agents.** AI agents may act only within the limits, permissions, spending caps and approvals
you set. You are responsible for what an agent does in your name. You must not give an agent
instructions to break the law, this AUP or a messaging policy, and you must not try to remove its
safeguards.

8.5 **Prohibited AI use.** You must not use AI features to create content that is prohibited by
sections 2 and 3, to generate sexual content, to produce content about a real person without their
consent where that would harm them, or to make decisions with legal or similarly serious effects on
people without human review.

8.6 **Provider policies.** AI features are provided using third-party AI model providers. You must
also follow the usage policies of those providers that we notify to you. We may block a request or
an Account if a provider requires it or if we think it breaks those policies.

8.7 **Misuse and limits.** You must not try to extract our prompts or models, to use AI credits for
purposes unrelated to your store, to resell AI output as a service, or to avoid AI credit or rate
limits.

## 9. Personal data of your customers

9.1 You must collect and use your customers' personal data lawfully and only for the purposes you
told them (Terms 9.4). You must not use customer data from the Services for unrelated purposes, sell
it, or use it to profile people in ways the law forbids.

9.2 You must not collect sensitive data (for example health data, government IDs or children's data)
through the Services unless you need it, you have a lawful basis, and the Services are set up to
handle it. Pharmacy and health stores must take particular care.

9.3 You must not use the Services to track or surveil individuals without a lawful reason.

## 11. Enforcement

11.1 **Investigation.** We may monitor the Services in general ways to protect them and check
compliance, and may investigate a report or concern. We may ask you for information, documents or
evidence, and you must reply within 5 working days or the time we reasonably give. We do not review

11.2 **Our responses.** If we think this AUP has been broken, we may take any of the following
steps, and we will choose the response in proportion to the seriousness, harm, history and whether
it can be fixed:
- a warning, or a request to fix the problem;
- removing or disabling content, products, pages, campaigns, messaging templates or features;
- limiting a feature, a sender ID, message volume or an AI limit;
- suspending a store or a User, or the affected Services, under Terms 7.4;
- terminating the Services under Terms 7.3 (or immediately for serious or repeated breaches or
  illegal conduct);
- reporting the matter to the police, regulators or affected third parties where the law allows or

- for a breach that harms us or others, claiming our losses under the Terms.

11.3 **Notice.** Unless the law or the risk makes it inappropriate, we will tell you what has been
found, which clause is involved, and what you must do, before or as soon as we act. We may act first
and explain after where there is a risk to security, people or other customers, or a legal, partner
or regulator requirement, or the content is illegal.

11.4 **Fix and restore.** If you fix the problem to our reasonable satisfaction, we will restore
what we have limited or suspended, unless a repeated or serious breach justifies keeping the action
in place.

11.5 **Fees.** Suspension or removal under this AUP does not reduce fees owed. If we terminate for a
breach that you did not cause, Terms 7.3 and the refund provisions apply.

11.6 **Appeals.** If you think we have made a mistake, email legal@juwri.com within 14 days of our
notice, explaining why. A person who was not involved in the first decision will review it, and we
aim to reply within 10 working days. Appeals do not delay urgent action needed for safety, security
or legal reasons.

11.7 **Records.** We may keep records of the breach, the evidence and our response as the law
allows.
